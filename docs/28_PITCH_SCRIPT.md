# Pitch script: 3 minutes, 11 slides, one film

Slot: 5 minutes. 3 minutes pitch (this script), 2 minutes jury questions. Target finish: **2:55**. Practise out loud with a timer; the film alone is 45 seconds, so you speak for about 2 minutes 10 seconds.

Rule from the challenge: no made-up numbers. This script has none. Wherever you do not know something, it says so out loud. That is the strong move, not the weak one.

Jury: Prashant Sinha (founder, SixHats.ai, entrepreneur and investor), Manan Khaneja (founder, Glace, ex-LG Ads), and the AI juror that scores on the same rubric: Problem and idea 20, Product works 25, Website + social + ad 20, Pitch and clarity 20, AI use and craft 15.

## Delivery notes

- **Stress words:** *ne* and *ko* on slide 2. *Confirm* on slide 4. *do not know yet* on slides 8 and 9.
- **Pauses:** one beat after "opposite directions of money". One full second of silence after the film ends before you speak.
- **No "revolutionise", no "passionate about".** Say what it does.
- Slide 2 is an illustration of a story, not a photo of a real shopkeeper. If asked, say so.
- If the film fails to play: say "The film is fictional and has no voice-over, so I will describe it: the shopkeeper cannot find the amount, then speaks, sees the parchi, taps, the stamp lands, the balance updates." Move on.

## The 8 hardest questions, with answers that are safe to say

Each answer uses only what is true today. The trap is the thing not to say.

**1. (Numbers) How big is the market?**
"I have not measured it, and I would rather say that than invent a number. What I can show is the problem, a working product, and a plan to talk to ten shops first."
*Trap: quoting a crore of kirana shops from memory without a source.*

**2. (Numbers) How much will they pay?**
"I do not know yet. The model is free to start, with a small plan for extras such as reminders. I have no pricing evidence, so the pricing test is the next step."
*Trap: naming a price like it is proven.*

**3. (User) Have you spoken to a shopkeeper?**
"Not yet. Everything you saw uses a fictional shop. The first ten shops, with consent, are the next step. I would rather learn from them than guess."
*Trap: implying friends or family counted as users.*

**4. (User) Who exactly is your user?**
"A kirana owner who gives credit to regular customers, with a busy counter and a phone with WhatsApp. In the demo he is the owner of 'Shree Ganesh Kirana', a fictional shop."
*Trap: "everyone with a shop".*

**5. (Competition) Khatabook already exists. Why you?**
"They exist and I respect that. I have not compared features, so I will not say what they lack. What I can say is what ours does: you speak, you read a parchi with the amount in digits and words, and nothing saves until you tap."
*Trap: claiming a competitor cannot do something we have not checked.*

**6. (Competition) Can someone copy this?**
"Yes, the idea can be copied. What is hard is trust: confirm before write, voided not deleted, drafts not auto-sent. That discipline is in the code and the tests, and it is what a shopkeeper needs before trusting money to an app."
*Trap: claiming a moat or a patent.*

**7. (How the AI works) What if it mishears?**
"Four steps. Speech becomes text. Our own rules read the sentence first, because common phrases are instant and work offline; if they are unsure, a language model, Gemini, proposes an entry. Then our code checks the amount, the customer and the direction. Then the owner sees a parchi and taps. If it mishears, he sees the wrong amount before anything saves, and can change it. Money is stored as whole paise, never as decimals."
*Trap: saying the AI is always right. Say: tap-before-save means a mistake is caught before it is saved.*

**8. (Why you) Why are you the person to build this?**
"I built every piece of this myself, with AI tools, in a short time: the product, the website, the posts, the film and this deck. I care that it is honest about what it does and does not know."
*Trap: claiming experience you do not have. If you have a personal reason (a family shop, a shopkeeper you know), say it in one sentence. It makes this answer far stronger.*

**Also likely**

- *Does it send WhatsApp messages by itself?* "No. It makes a draft; WhatsApp opens and you press Send."
- *Is the data safe?* "Each shop's data is separate, sessions can be revoked, entries are voided not deleted, and you can export or erase your data. I have not run a production restore test or off-site backups yet."
- *Is the sample real?* "No. Every name and amount in the sample is fictional."

**The honest way to say "I don't know yet":** "I do not know yet. Here is how I would find out: ..." and then name the test.
